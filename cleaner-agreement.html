3. How the Platform Works for Cleaners

CleanSnap is a marketplace platform that connects Customers with independent Cleaners. We facilitate bookings, payments, and communication — but we do not provide cleaning services ourselves.

  1. Job Offers: When a Customer requests a clean, the job is offered to available Cleaners in the area. You may view available jobs in the app.
  2. Acceptance: You choose which jobs to accept. You are under no obligation to accept any job.
  3. Performing the Service: Once you accept a job, you travel to the Customer's location and perform the cleaning service to a professional standard.
  4. Completion and Payment: When you mark a job as complete, the Customer's payment is captured and your earnings (minus the platform service fee) are recorded. Your earnings are then transferred to you after a short holding period — see Section 6, Payment Timing.

4. Your Right to Accept or Decline Work

You have the absolute right to:

  • Accept or decline any job offered to you, for any reason, without penalty (subject to the cancellation terms below).
  • Set your own availability through the app. You decide when you are available to work.
  • Take breaks or time off at any time, without permission or notice to CleanSnap.
  • Work for other platforms or clients simultaneously — there is no exclusivity requirement.

We do not set minimum hours, shifts, or quotas. Your engagement with the Platform is entirely at your discretion.

5. Returning Work; Substitution

You are never obliged to perform an accepted job personally. If you are unable or unwilling to carry out an accepted job, you may release it through the app at any time, without penalty and without giving a reason. A released job returns to the open job pool, where any other verified Cleaner may claim it, and the Customer is notified.

If you wish a specific other Cleaner to take over a job, you may release it and arrange for that Cleaner to claim it from the pool, or contact us and we will assist with the handover. For the safety of Customers, anyone performing work arranged through the Platform must themselves be a registered and verified Cleaner (identity, background check, and insurance) — this is a customer-safety vetting requirement, not a requirement of personal service by you. As section 11 requires of every Cleaner, they must also have shown us their right to work in the United Kingdom before they can claim any job.

If you cannot fulfil an accepted job, please release it as early as possible so the Customer can be matched with another Cleaner.

6. How You Are Paid

Booking Price and Platform Service Fee

The total booking price paid by the Customer is made up of two parts: your price for the cleaning service (85% of the booking total) and CleanSnap's platform service fee (15% of the booking total), which is charged to the Customer as part of the total they pay. The service fee is charged to the Customer, not to you: you do not pay CleanSnap any fee for finding work.

For example: if the total booking price is £100, the Customer pays £100 — £85 is your price for the service and is transferred to you, and £15 is the platform service fee paid by the Customer to CleanSnap.

Booking totals are proposed by the Platform's quoting system, and you may propose a different price for any job before accepting it (see the app's price-offer feature). You are always free to decline any job whose price you consider inadequate.

Chargebacks and Payment Reversals

If a Customer's payment for a booking is reversed after your earnings have been transferred (for example, a card chargeback, or a refund issued under the complaints process), we may reverse the corresponding transfer to your account. If this happens, we will notify you promptly with the reason, and you may dispute it as set out in Section 18.

Payment Method

Earnings are paid to the bank account you register with Stripe during payment setup. CleanSnap never sees or stores your bank details. You are responsible for ensuring your payment details are accurate and up to date.

Payment Timing

Your earnings are recorded as soon as you mark a job complete. They are transferred to your account approximately 24 hours later. This short holding period exists so that a problem raised by the Customer on the day of the clean can be settled between us and the Customer, rather than by taking money back out of your account after you have received it.

After that, the time it takes to reach your bank depends on Stripe's processing schedule and your bank. We aim to transfer earnings promptly, but CleanSnap is not responsible for delays caused by Stripe or your bank.

Tax Responsibility

As a self-employed contractor, you are solely responsible for:

  • Registering as self-employed with HMRC
  • Filing your own Self Assessment tax return
  • Paying income tax and National Insurance contributions
  • Keeping accurate records of your earnings and expenses
  • Registering for VAT if your turnover exceeds the VAT threshold

CleanSnap does not deduct tax or National Insurance from your earnings. We are required to report your identity and earnings to HMRC under the Platform Operators (Due Diligence and Reporting Requirements) Regulations 2023 (the UK's digital-platform reporting rules, sometimes known as "DAC7").

9. Cancellations by You

If you need to cancel an accepted job:

  • Cancel as early as possible through the app to allow the Customer to make alternative arrangements.
  • When you cancel, the job is automatically returned to the job pool and offered to other available Cleaners, and the Customer is notified.
  • The Customer is not charged a cancellation fee because of your cancellation, and is given a relaxed window to cancel free of charge (up to 12 hours before the scheduled time) if they prefer not to proceed with a replacement Cleaner.

Note on Customer cancellations: if a Customer cancels late and a cancellation fee applies after you have been assigned, you receive 50% of that fee in recognition of your reserved time, paid to your connected account.

Note on Customer changes: a Customer can move a booking you have accepted, but only while cancelling it would still be free to them: more than 48 hours before the scheduled start time, or more than 12 hours where the Customer's previously assigned Cleaner cancelled and the job was re-offered to you. Inside that window they must cancel instead, and the fee above applies. The same distance applies to the new time, so a job cannot be pulled forward onto you at shorter notice than the one you accepted it with, and it cannot be moved onto a time you are already booked for. If a booking of yours is moved, we notify you with the time it was and the time it now is. You are not obliged to keep it: if the new time does not suit you, cancel it and the job returns to the pool as above.

Repeated cancellations may result in:

  • Reduced visibility of your profile in search results
  • Temporary suspension from receiving new job offers
  • In severe cases, termination of your account

We understand that genuine emergencies occur. Occasional cancellations with reasonable notice will not result in penalties.

How the first two of those happen, and how they end

The first two can happen automatically, so you should know what triggers them. We keep two figures about the jobs you accept: the share you complete, and the share you start within the time slot you agreed. If either falls below half — each counted only once there are at least three jobs to judge it on, so a single bad day never triggers it — you stop appearing in search results and stop being offered new work.

We tell you when this happens, and which of the two figures caused it. It is not a suspension of your account and not a termination: you keep your login, you keep every job you have already accepted, and carrying those out is what brings the figure back up and restores you automatically. Jobs a Customer cancelled, jobs whose price you and the Customer did not agree, jobs where the Customer was not there, and jobs closed by us do not count against you. If you think a figure is wrong, write to support@cleansnap.co.uk and we will look at it with you.

Not attending a job you have accepted

Releasing a job never costs you money, at any time and for any reason — that does not change, and it is why this next paragraph exists. It is not free of every consequence: a job you release counts in the completion figure described above, and repeated cancellations can have the effects listed there. But you are never charged for one. This paragraph is about the different case: not carrying out a job, not releasing it, and not reporting that the Customer was not there.

If the time slot you accepted ends and you have neither started the job, nor released it, nor reported that the Customer was not there (see "The Customer is not there" below), the Customer pays nothing at all — no fee, no charge, because nobody came. We charge you a fee equal to the platform service fee we would have received for that booking, which is 15% of the booking total. We set it against your next earnings; if none are due, it is held against your account until some are. You will be told when this happens and for which booking.

The reason for it is the difference between the two situations. Releasing a job takes one tap and returns it to the pool, where another Cleaner can pick it up and the Customer is still looked after. A job nobody attends and nobody released leaves a Customer waiting at home for someone who never comes, and leaves us with no way to help them in time.

Genuine emergencies. If you could not attend and could not release the job for reasons outside your control — illness, an accident, a family emergency — tell us and we will waive this fee. Write to support@cleansnap.co.uk within 14 days of the job. We will not ask you to prove a private matter; we ask only that you tell us.

This fee does not apply to a job you carried out, nor to one you released, however close to the start time you released it, nor to one on which you reported that the Customer was not there and we did not find the report untrue.

The Customer is not there

Sometimes you arrive and nobody lets you in, or the access the Customer arranged — a key, a code — does not work. This is a third case, and it is neither of the two above: you came, and the job could not be done. Arrive inside the time slot, then tell the Customer you are there through the app — the app sends them a message and a notification and records the time — call the number if the app shows one, and wait at least 15 minutes. If you still cannot get in — whether or not the Customer has answered — tell us through the app that the Customer is not there and take a photograph of the entrance. You can do this until 15 minutes after the slot ends, so that arriving in its last minutes and waiting the full 15 does not cost you the report. The app records the time and your location when you arrive and when you report; it needs your location for this purpose only, at those two moments, never in the background, and the report cannot be made without it. The report needs a connection: if you have none at the door, move to where you have one — the location recorded is the one at the moment you tap.

You may instead hand the job back, as sections 5 and 9 allow at any time: that costs you nothing and the Customer pays nothing, but it counts in your completion figure and no share of any fee is due to you. Report only if you were at the property inside the slot and the Customer was not: every report is checked, and an untrue one is dishonest behaviour under section 15.

Once you have reported, the job is over for you: you do not wait for the slot to end, and the fee for not attending does not apply to you, because you attended. The Customer is charged 50% of the booking total when you report, exactly as if they had cancelled after the start time, and is told at once. A member of our team reviews every report — where you were and when, when you told the Customer you had arrived, and the photograph — and aims to decide within 5 business days. If we uphold it, you receive half of what the Customer paid — 25% of the booking total — transferred to your connected account once we have decided, not when you report; the job counts against neither of the two figures in this section. We tell you the outcome.

If our review finds that you were not at the property inside the slot, the report is refused: the Customer is refunded in full, and the paragraphs above on not attending apply as if no report had been made. If we excuse the Customer for an emergency outside their control, the Customer is refunded in full and you still receive 25% of the booking total, paid by CleanSnap, because you came. In every other case where we do not uphold the report, the Customer is refunded in full, you are neither paid nor charged anything for the job, and it counts against neither figure. If the Customer answers within 14 days and we change our decision after your share has been transferred, the share is taken back and you are told why, with the right to respond in section 18 (see also section 6, Chargebacks and Payment Reversals). The report needs a payment held for the booking: if the Customer has not yet paid an approved new price, the app tells you before you set off, and you should not travel.

11. Identity and Verification

To register as a Cleaner, you must:

  • Be at least 18 years old
  • Have the legal right to work in the United Kingdom, and show it to us before you can accept jobs (see below)
  • Provide accurate personal information, including your full legal name and your National Insurance number, which is required to register
  • Complete any identity verification or background check processes that we require

How we check your right to work. Before you can accept any job we confirm your right to work in the United Kingdom. UK immigration law requires this of us from 1 October 2026: the Immigration, Asylum and Nationality Act 2006, as extended to online matching services by section 48 of the Border Security, Asylum and Immigration Act 2025, treats a platform like CleanSnap as your employer for this purpose. There are two routes. If you are not a British or Irish citizen, generate a share code on gov.uk ("Prove your right to work to an employer") and enter it in the app; a member of our team checks it, with the date of birth you gave us when you registered, on the government service ("Check a job applicant's right to work: use their share code"), confirms on a short video call that we arrange through the app that the photograph on the government record is you — no software compares faces, and the call is not recorded — and records the outcome, the date of the check and, if your permission to work is for a limited time, the date it ends. A share code is valid for 90 days from the day it is generated. If you are a British or Irish citizen you have no share code: your British passport, or your Irish passport or passport card, current or expired, is the proof — but the law lets us accept it only by seeing the original, with you present, in person or on a video call while we hold the document you have sent us (returned by tracked post), or, once we offer it, through a digital verification service registered with the government, which checks the passport and that it is yours from your device (we tell you in the app when it is available, and the Privacy Policy names the provider before we use one); a photograph of your passport uploaded in the app tells us who you are, but on its own it is not proof of your right to work, and a driving licence shows who you are but not that you may work here. If you are British or Irish and have no passport, write to support@cleansnap.co.uk before you upload anything: the Home Office accepts other documents — a birth or adoption certificate issued in the UK, the Channel Islands, the Isle of Man or Ireland, or a certificate of registration or naturalisation as a British citizen, in each case together with an official document from a government agency or a previous employer showing your name and your permanent National Insurance number — and we check the originals in the same way. You are never treated less favourably for proving your right to work one way rather than another. We keep a copy of the page the government service shows us, or of the documents we checked, together with the outcome and the date, for as long as you are registered with us and for two years after your account is closed, because the law requires whoever makes the check to keep that evidence; the Privacy Policy says how. Your share code itself is kept, encrypted, only until the check is made. If your permission to work ends on a date, we remind you in the app and by email before that date so that you can generate a new share code and we can repeat the check; from that date, until a new check has been recorded, you cannot accept jobs, as with insurance that has lapsed (section 10), and we tell you when this happens. If you were verified before this version of the Agreement, you cannot accept new jobs until we have confirmed your right to work; the app tells you what to do.

We reserve the right to request additional documentation at any time to verify your identity and right to work, including documentary right-to-work checks required of platforms engaging self-employed contractors under UK immigration law. Failure to provide requested documentation may result in suspension or termination of your account.
